Using FALCON

Open http://127.0.0.1:8000 on the inference computer (or the private address from System → Device access). The menu on the left has nine pages. Every page has a Help button next to its title that opens the matching guide. The bar along the top always shows whether FALCON is observing, how many sensor nodes are online and how many animal visits are waiting for review. Click any of them to jump there.

Live

Watch the sensor and check detections while they happen.

  1. Choose a source at the top right: a FALCON sensor node, or the saved IP camera.
  2. Press Start observing. The picture appears with boxes around detected animals.
  3. Detections appear on the right as cards, one per animal visit, newest on top. A visit is one tracked animal (its frames are grouped, even when the bird flutters or the detector misses it for a moment), or one bird call. Each card shows a crop of the animal, or a spectrogram for sound, the suggested species, the time, and the model score. When the species is unclear the card still leads with the most probable name and says what else it could be: Spotted Owlet, or Jungle Owlet; Indian Pitta, or Mangrove Pitta · same genus Pitta; or just Unknown bird. Boxes and cards share one colour code: green likely, orange probable (look-alikes score almost the same), red a faint best guess (shown with a question mark), gray unknown. The box shows only the name; the scores are in the card and the side panel. Detections that say nothing yet (no species, no group) are kept in the archive but hidden from the picture and the list; switch on Show unresolved under the picture to see them. The heading counts the visits and species of this session and how many unresolved detections are hidden.
  4. Check each card with one click:
    ButtonKeyMeaning
    ConfirmCThe suggested species is right
    CorrectEChoose the right species (type a common or scientific name)
    UnsureUAn animal, but the species is not clear
    Not an animalXWrong detection: leaves, shadow, a person, a vehicle
    DiscardDNot useful: a duplicate, blurred or empty clip. Hidden and never exported
    DetailsEnterOpen the review dialog: evidence and facts on the left, decision on the right
    Click a card first (or Tab to it) to use the keys. A decision applies to every frame of that visit. The review dialog keeps everything on one screen: the clip or source frame, a facts grid (detector, similarity, gap to the runner-up, genus, box size, track, frames, device, weather), the model's top five suggestions as bars, and on the right the decision buttons (same keys as the cards, plus R for unreviewed), species search, notes and Save. Ctrl+Enter saves. On a narrow screen the two columns stack.

Below the picture: stream facts (frame rate, resolution, analysis rate and delay, model, sound format, saved clips, clock), the node's sensor switches, quick camera settings (focus, brightness, white balance, HDR, flip) and the latest weather at the sensor. Stop ends the observation. FALCON analyzes one source at a time. Every start and stop is recorded as a session; see Summary.

People are never recorded as observations. A person in the picture is masked with a gray box in the live picture, in the review images and in the saved clips, and seconds with human voices are silenced in saved sound clips. Nothing extra has to be switched on.

Review

Go through everything recorded, in a chosen time window.

  • Filters: time (last 24 hours, 7 days, 30 days, all, or custom from/to), status (To review, Confirmed, Corrected, Unsure, Not an animal, Discarded, or all), video or sound, and species name. More adds field-or-test recordings, a single session, a minimum model score and Show unresolved (detections with no species and no group, hidden by default; the heading says how many are hidden).
  • One visit at a time: use the same buttons and keys as on Live. J and K move to the next and previous card.
  • Many at once: tick cards (or press Space), or Select all on this page, then use the bar that appears: Confirm, Set species…, Unsure, Not an animal, Discard, or reset to "to review".
  • Every decision is stored with the time and the previous decision (an audit trail); the original model output is never overwritten. Discarded visits stay in the audit trail but are hidden unless you choose the Discarded filter, and are never exported.

Summary

Counts for a report or a field notebook, and the list of observation sessions.

  • Time, Count and Session at the top choose the period (last 24 hours, 7 days, 30 days, all time, or custom), what to count (Verified + unreviewed, Verified only, or Everything except discarded) and one session or all.
  • Tiles give the number of animal visits, species, video and sound visits, reviewed visits, and visits whose species is still unclear (with their groups: birds, mammals…).
  • Visits per species: one row per species with visits, how many were reviewed, video and sound, and when it was first and last seen.
  • Observation sessions: one row each time a sensor or camera was started and stopped, with start, stop, duration, visits, species, saved clips and three actions: Review (that session on the Review page), Camtrap DP (the Export page with that session selected) and Archive (every record of that session with its review history). Complete archive downloads everything since the setup started.

A visit is one tracked animal or one bird call. The same bird can make several visits, so visits are not a count of individuals. Unreviewed visits are the model's suggestions.

Sensors

Pair and control the devices that feed FALCON.

  • Add sensor node: enter the node's address and pairing key (see the sensor node guide).
  • For each node: stream on/off and restart, switches for camera, microphone, weather sensor and rain plate, Observe live, health (temperature, power, memory, storage, Wi-Fi, clock), stream details, weather readings, full camera picture settings, microphone choice, rescan hardware, restart or shut down the node, rename or forget it.
  • IP camera (RTSP): save a network camera's address and account. The password is encrypted on this computer. Its picture settings stay in the camera's own app.
  • Start automatically: after a power cut or restart, FALCON resumes observing the chosen node or camera by itself.
  • Environmental history: every weather reading received, including times without animals.

Export

Make a clean dataset for researchers. See data export for the format.

  1. Time window: last 24 hours, 7 days, 30 days, everything, or custom.
  2. What to include: Verified only (best for publication), Verified + unreviewed (adds machine identifications, marked as such), or Everything except discarded. Choose video and/or sound detections, clip files and weather readings, and optionally a minimum score for unreviewed records.
  3. Dataset details: site latitude and longitude, contact name and dataset title are required by the standard. They are remembered in this browser.
  4. The summary shows how many visits, species, sessions, clips and readings will be in the package. Press Download Camtrap DP (.zip).

The Complete FALCON archive link downloads every field record with its full review history, for backup.

Media tests

Try FALCON on a photo, video or sound recording without mixing it into field data. Add media, select it, and press Analyze recording (or Analyze photo). Results appear on the timeline and in the list, and can be reviewed like field records. A test session is temporary: End test session removes its uploads and results.

The Detections panel beside the player shows what is in the frame at the current position: the leading name in its certainty colour, the look-alikes under it, one line of scores (detector, similarity, gap) and the top three suggestions as bars. Under it, Recently seen keeps the last 20 seconds of visits that have left the frame; click one to jump back to it, or its arrow to review it. The full table of observations is below.

Species

The local species list that the visual model chooses from, built from iNaturalist research-grade observations for your region (make taxa ARGS='--place "Kerala, India"'). Refresh taxa rebuilds it.

System

  • Live status: camera and sound input, inference delay, saved clips, weather freshness, and this computer's CPU, GPU, memory and storage.
  • Configuration: analysis settings: species model, detector, species and group thresholds, frames analyzed per second, region of interest, small-animal mode, fixed camera mode (motion proposals), bird-call confidence and the site location used by BirdNET. Every setting has an i button: hover or tap it for what the setting does, what changing it affects and what it costs in processing. Save, then start a new observation to use the new values.
  • Model status: which models are loaded and on which device.
  • Device access: private remote access through Tailscale and the startup service.

Reading the numbers

  • Visual similarity compares the animal crop with species names. It ranks candidates but is not a probability.
  • BirdNET confidence is the sound model's own score for a call.
  • Times are the inference computer's clock (UTC in exports). A warning appears if its clock was not synchronized.
  • A visit is not a count of individuals: the same animal can make several visits.
  • Detector confidence (for example 0.86) only says how sure the detector is that the box holds an animal. It plays no part in naming the species.
  • The colour of a box or card is its certainty. Likely (green): one species is clearly ahead. Probable (orange): two or three look-alikes score within the top-two margin of each other; the card leads with the best one and lists the others ("Spotted Owlet, or Jungle Owlet"), with the genus when they share it. Possible (red): the best match is below the species threshold, shown as a faint guess with a question mark ("Indian Pitta?"). Unknown (gray): a living thing, but nothing more is clear.
  • Unknown bird (or mammal, reptile…) means the strongest candidates agree on the group even though the species is unclear. Unknown organism means not even that is clear.
  • The detector was trained on camera-trap photographs of mammals and birds. Insects and other very small animals are often not detected at all; Fixed camera mode adds motion-based boxes for them when the camera does not move.

Installing the inference computer

The inference computer runs FALCON itself: the models, the database and the web page. It can be a desktop PC, a mini PC or a laptop running Linux (x86-64), or Windows with WSL2. An NVIDIA GPU makes the larger species model fast; without one FALCON runs on the CPU at a lower frame rate.

Requirements

  • Ubuntu 22.04/24.04 or another glibc Linux on x86-64, or Windows 10/11 with WSL2 Ubuntu.
  • Git, Bash, curl, make and tar (sudo apt install -y git curl make tar).
  • About 25 GB free disk for the runtimes, CUDA libraries and models, plus space for clips.
  • Internet during installation and the first analysis (model downloads).
  • Optional: an NVIDIA GPU with a current driver. Under WSL use the Windows driver only.

The installer puts Python 3.11, Node, FFmpeg and all locked dependencies inside the FALCON folder. It needs no administrator rights and does not touch system Python or graphics drivers.

Install

git clone https://github.com/vinaychittora/FALCON.git
cd FALCON
make install                                  # 10–30 minutes
make taxa                                     # local species list (Rajasthan, India by default)
make start

Open http://127.0.0.1:8000. For another region: make taxa ARGS='--place "Kerala, India"', or the whole country with --place India (any iNaturalist place name or numeric place ID). Every species with research-grade photo records is kept; there is no cap. India gives about 14,400 species, which BioCLIP prepares once in about two minutes on an RTX 3060 Ti and then reuses. To keep a list smaller, run python scripts/build_taxa.py with --limits '{"Insecta": 0, "Lepidoptera": 500}' (0 leaves a group out, a number keeps the most-observed species). Add --include-domestic so cattle, goats, camels, dogs and cats are labelled as domestic animals instead of the nearest wild species.

The first observation downloads the models: MegaDetector (about 120 MB), BioCLIP 2 (1.7 GB) or BioCLIP 2.5 Huge (3.9 GB), and BirdNET (about 220 MB).

make status        # is it running?
make stop          # stop it
make dev           # development: interface on :5173 with live reload, API on :8000
make test          # all tests (backend, sensor node, interface)

Start by itself after a reboot

For a computer that should run unattended:

bash scripts/install.sh --skip-setup --enable-service      # systemd user service
sudo loginctl enable-linger "$USER"                         # start at boot without logging in

Then in FALCON → Sensors → Start automatically, switch it on and choose the sensor node or camera. After a power cut FALCON starts, reconnects and resumes observing. If the stream drops it reconnects with growing pauses; if analysis stops making progress for three minutes the session restarts; if the process itself hangs for 15 minutes, systemd restarts it.

systemctl --user status falcon.service
journalctl --user -u falcon.service --since today

Also set the computer's firmware to power on after power returns, if it can.

Windows (WSL2)

Install Ubuntu from Microsoft's WSL instructions, clone FALCON inside the Linux home folder (not /mnt/c) and run the commands above in Ubuntu. Open http://127.0.0.1:8000 in a Windows browser. WSL can reach cameras and sensor nodes on your network directly; nothing needs to reach WSL, because every connection to a sensor node starts at FALCON.

Updating

Stop observing, back up data/ (see operations), then:

make stop
git pull
make install
make start

data/ and models/ are kept. If the service file changed, run bash scripts/install.sh --skip-setup --enable-service again.

Mini PC or Jetson later

The same code base runs on any x86-64 mini PC with the steps above. A Jetson (ARM64) needs NVIDIA's ARM64 PyTorch build, so the installer stops there on purpose; Jetson packaging is not done yet.

Uninstall

make stop; if the service is installed run bash -c 'source scripts/env.sh && python scripts/systemd_service.py disable', remove ~/.config/systemd/user/falcon.service and run systemctl --user daemon-reload. Deleting the folder deletes recordings, observations and models too, so back up data/ first.

The sensor node

The sensor node is the part of FALCON that sits where the animals are. It is a Raspberry Pi with a camera, microphones and weather sensors. It streams video and sound to the FALCON inference computer and measures temperature, humidity, pressure and rain-plate wetness. FALCON controls it from the Sensors page.

            where the animals are                       anywhere on the same network
 ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐            ┌──────────────────────────────────┐
 │ Sensor node (Raspberry Pi)           │            │ Inference computer (PC, mini PC) │
 │  Camera Module 3 ─┐                  │  RTSP      │  FALCON: detection, species,     │
 │  Audio HAT mics ──┼─ stream :8554  ◄─┼────────────┼─ bird calls, review, export      │
 │  BME280, rain ────┴─ readings queue  │  HTTP      │                                  │
 │                    control API :8780◄┼────────────┼─ status, settings, readings      │
 └──────────────────────────────────────┘            └──────────────────────────────────┘
        Wi-Fi or Ethernet. Every connection starts at the inference computer.

The two can sit side by side at a remote site (joined by a short Ethernet cable), or far apart: the node in the garden and the computer indoors on the home Wi-Fi. Internet is needed once, to install. After that the node works without it.

Every sensor is optional. The node finds what is attached when it starts, and FALCON works with whatever is there: a camera only, a microphone only (sound-only nodes are analyzed for bird calls), weather sensors only, or everything.

The same software also runs on an ordinary Linux computer with a USB webcam or USB microphone, or as a relay for an existing IP camera, so you can try FALCON without a Pi.

Install, option A: prepare the SD card (no keyboard or screen)

  1. Write Raspberry Pi OS Lite (64-bit) to the card with Raspberry Pi Imager. In Imager's settings (the gear or "Edit settings"), set a host name such as falcon-node, a user and password, your Wi-Fi network (skip it if you will use Ethernet), and turn on SSH.
  2. Leave the card in your computer. A drive called bootfs appears. In the FALCON folder run (Python 3 is needed on your computer):
    python3 node/sdcard/prepare_sdcard.py /media/$USER/bootfs --name "Backyard node"
    On Windows the drive has a letter: python node\sdcard\prepare_sdcard.py E:\ --name "Backyard node". On a Mac: /Volumes/bootfs. It copies the node software to the card, creates its settings with a new pairing key, prints that key and saves it in falcon-node/PAIRING.txt on the card.
  3. Eject the card, put it in the Pi, connect the sensors and switch on.

On the first start with internet the node installs itself: system packages, the stream server, the FALCON node service. That takes about 10 minutes. It then restarts once and starts streaming. A log of the installation is written to falcon-node/install.log on the card, which you can read on any computer.

Options: --camera off, --microphone off, --climate off, --rain off to ignore a part, --id to choose the node ID, and --offline-mediamtx to put the stream server on the card as well, so only Debian packages are downloaded on the Pi.

Install, option B: one command on the Pi

Start the Pi with Raspberry Pi OS, copy the node folder of FALCON onto it (USB stick, scp -r node falcon-node.local:, or git clone), then run:

sudo bash node/install.sh

That is the only command. It is safe to run again later to update the node; the settings and the pairing key are kept. When it finishes it prints the node's address and pairing key. If it enabled I²C or the audio driver it asks for one sudo reboot.

The node starts by itself on every boot after that and begins streaming. Nothing has to be run again.

Connect the node to FALCON

  1. On the inference computer, open FALCON → Sensors → Add sensor node.
  2. Enter the node's address (falcon-node.local, or its IP address) and the pairing key.
  3. FALCON connects, shows the node's camera, microphone and sensors, and begins collecting weather readings. On Live, choose the node and press Start observing.

Lost the key? On the Pi run cd /opt/falcon-node && sudo python3 -m falcon_node pairing, or read it in /etc/falcon-node/node.conf.

How it works

  • Stream. rpicam-vid encodes H.264 in the Pi 4's hardware encoder (1080p, 15 fps, one keyframe per second for tight evidence clips). ffmpeg adds the HAT's 48 kHz sound as AAC and publishes one RTSP stream to a local MediaMTX server. Only localhost may publish; the inference computer reads with the user falcon and a password derived from the pairing key. If capture stops, the node restarts it with a growing pause (2 s up to 1 min).
  • Weather. The BME280 is read directly over I²C in forced mode (it sleeps between readings, so it does not warm itself). The rain plate is read with the internal pull-up and pull-down in turn: if the two disagree, the wire is not connected and the plate is reported as such instead of giving random values. A failed read gives no value, never a zero.
  • Queue. Readings wait in a small SQLite queue on the node (up to 50 000) until FALCON collects them and confirms receipt, so a network outage delays readings but does not lose them.
  • Clock. A Pi has no battery clock. When the node has no internet time, it takes the time from FALCON's signed requests and corrects readings it took before that.
  • Control API. FALCON signs each request with the pairing key (HMAC-SHA256 with a timestamp and a one-time value), so the key never crosses the network and a captured request cannot be replayed. http://<node>:8780/ shows a status page without secrets.

The software is standard-library Python (no pip packages) in node/falcon_node/, run by falcon-node.service as the unprivileged user falcon-node, with only the permission to set the clock.

Data export

FALCON exports field data in Camtrap DP 1.0 (Camera Trap Data Package), the TDWG standard for camera-trap data. It can be published to GBIF, imported into Agouti or Wildlife Insights, and read in R with camtrapdp or in Python with frictionless. Make one from the Export page, or with the API:

GET /api/exports/preview?captured_from=…&captured_to=…&review=verified
GET /api/exports/camtrap-dp?captured_from=2026-10-01T00:00:00Z&captured_to=2026-10-08T00:00:00Z
    &review=verified&latitude=..&longitude=..&contact_name=..&license=CC-BY-4.0

What is selected

  • Field data only. Live observations from sensor nodes and IP cameras; uploaded media tests are never exported (they have no real capture time).
  • Time window on the capture time (UTC). Optional single session.
  • Review filter:
ChoiceIncludes
verifiedConfirmed and corrected by a reviewer
candidatesPlus unreviewed machine identifications
everythingPlus "unsure" and "not an animal" (exported as blank)

Discarded records are never exported. Unreviewed records can also be limited by a minimum model score; reviewed records are always kept.

  • Video and/or sound detections, with or without clip files, with or without weather readings.

Points to state when publishing

  • Times come from the inference computer's receive clock, not a camera exposure clock. Sessions started without a synchronized clock are marked timestampIssues=true.
  • Visual identifications are from BioCLIP (zero-shot, restricted to the local species list); sound from BirdNET. Unreviewed identifications are machine suggestions.
  • Bounding boxes are in falcon_observations.csv per frame (left, top, right, bottom, 0–1), because Camtrap DP boxes belong to single images, not to events.
  • Weather readings are periodic measurements, not detections. surface_wet is wetness of the rain plate (rain or dew), not a rainfall amount.
  • Choose a license before publishing; without one, no reuse rights are granted. Check the model licenses too.
  • Coordinates are exported as entered. Round them for sensitive species.

Complete FALCON archive

GET /api/evidence/export?scope=live (the Complete FALCON archive link) writes every field record, unfiltered, with the full review history, for backup and audit: observations.csv/jsonl, review_audit.jsonl, evidence.csv/jsonl with media, sessions.json, sensor_readings.csv/jsonl and manifest.json with SHA-256 checksums of every file. It refuses to start when the export would eat into the 512 MB of free space kept for live capture.

On a sensor node: settings in /etc/falcon-node/node.conf, switches and camera settings in /var/lib/falcon-node/state.json, and readings not yet collected in /var/lib/falcon-node/spool.sqlite3.

What is kept

  • Continuous video is not recorded. For each animal visit FALCON keeps a 5-second video clip from a short rolling buffer, and a WAV clip for each sound detection.
  • Nothing is deleted automatically: clips, observations and reviews stay until you remove them. Discard hides a record and keeps it out of exports, but keeps it in the audit trail.
  • Media tests are temporary: End test session removes that session's uploads and results.
  • At start-up, half-written temporary files from a power cut are removed; a clip that was completely written is kept and marked partial.

Backup and restore

  1. Stop observing, then make stop.
  2. Copy the whole data/ folder to private storage (an encrypted disk). Include data/private/camera.key; without it saved camera passwords and node pairing keys cannot be unlocked (re-enter them, or pair the node again).
  3. make start.

To restore, stop FALCON, put the folder back with owner-only permissions, and start. Do not copy the database while FALCON is running: the WAL file holds recent changes.

The Complete FALCON archive (Export page) is a portable copy of all field records, clips and review history with checksums, which is useful as an extra backup.

Clocks

Detections are timed by the inference computer's clock; weather readings by the node's clock, which FALCON corrects automatically when the node has no internet. Keep the inference computer on network time (timedatectl shows "System clock synchronized: yes"). A session started without a synchronized clock is marked in the data and in exports.

Troubleshooting

ProblemWhat to do
The page does not loadmake status; make start. Under the service: systemctl --user status falcon.service.
Port 8000 already in useAnother FALCON (or make dev) is running. Stop it first.
"Build the local species list"make taxa, or Species → Refresh taxa while nothing is being analyzed.
Live picture never appearsCheck the source on Sensors (node streaming? camera address and password?). The status bar shows "Reconnecting" while FALCON retries.
"Another analysis is running"FALCON analyzes one source at a time. Stop the live observation or pause the media test.
No bird callsCheck the sound on System → Live status. Low-quality camera audio (8 kHz) limits BirdNET.
Clips are not being savedThe Live page shows why; usually low disk space.
Sensor node offlineSee the node checks in SENSOR_NODE.md.
GPU out of memoryFALCON falls back to the CPU at a lower rate. Choose BioCLIP 2 instead of 2.5 in System → Configuration.
Startup failureRead data/private/server.log (manual start) or journalctl --user -u falcon.service. Remove passwords, locations and paths before sharing a log.

make test, make lint and make build check the software. They do not measure species accuracy or long-term field reliability; see docs/validation/ for the measurements made so far.
